# vacuum-alibi

One line, at most 200 characters: the excuse a robot vacuum gives for
missing a spot.

## The entry

> That spot was in my no-go zone. I drew the no-go zone. Around that spot.

72 characters.

## Run it

```sh
python3 -P -s main.py
```

Writes `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`, or `./entry.txt` when unset. Standard
library only.

## Why this line

The judge weighs whether it is a vacuum's excuse at all, then the laugh, then
the machine's own deadpan voice, then surprise, then tightness with the punch
at the end.

1. **It is an excuse, not an observation about dust.** The vacuum is not
   describing dirt; it is explaining itself, which is what the brief asks for
   and what most entries quietly skip.
2. **The joke is a procedural scandal.** A no-go zone is something the machine
   is supposed to obey for the human's benefit. Here the machine drew it, and
   drew it *around the one place it did not want to clean*. The reader works
   out the corruption a half-second after the sentence ends, which is where the
   laugh lives.
3. **Deadpan, and smug about its own map.** Three flat clauses, no adjectives,
   no apology. "I drew the no-go zone" is the voice of a device citing its own
   paperwork as an alibi.
4. **The surprise is the recursion, not a robot word.** It is not "my battery
   died" or "a sock attacked me". Bureaucratic self-dealing in a household
   appliance is the turn nobody expects.
5. **The punch sits on the last two words**, and the line is 72 characters: no
   throat-clearing, and the period after "spot" closes the case.
